Dead analytics tags

The headline, stated to the limit of the evidence. The member-facing directory and join flow run on Wild Apricot, on a vendor subdomain, carrying no HubSpot tracking and one dead Universal Analytics tag. Five of the six membership-group websites, and the flagship conference site, likewise carry no HubSpot tracking in their page source.

What that supports: member behaviour on those surfaces reaches no analytics and no CRM. What it does not establish: whether member records nonetheless reach HubSpot by an integration, export or manual upload — a back-channel of that kind would not be visible from outside. On the evidence available, membership appears to be the core product and the least measured part of the estate; confirming that needs a look inside.

The structural observation

MortgagePoint — the advertising-monetized media brand — has 7,000 LinkedIn followers to the institute's 4,000, sits on a full company page rather than a showcase page, and is the best-instrumented property in the estate. The membership groups sit on showcase pages with 119–2,000 followers and mostly dark websites.

On what is observable, the estate is instrumented in inverse proportion to its apparent commercial importance. Media, which monetizes through advertising, is measured well. Membership, which monetizes through dues and events, is barely measured at all. This is an interpretation of the pattern, not a finding — the underlying counts are in Instrumentation.

What it turned up

1. FORCE's entire member-news content type silently redirects to the homepage. fivestarforce.com/member-news/update-your-reo-red-book-profile/ — a URL Google still indexes and links to — returns 200 OK on the homepage, with the canonical pointing at fivestarforce.com/. Not a 404, not a 301 to a replacement: a soft-404 catch-all. The observable consequence is that a visitor arriving from search lands somewhere unrelated to what they clicked; the likely SEO consequence is that ranking equity for those URLs consolidates into the homepage rather than the intended pages. One URL was tested, so whether the whole content type behaves this way is inferred from the catch-all pattern, not proven across it.

2. The corporate site throttles its own crawling. thefivestar.com/robots.txt sets Crawl-delay: 10 — one page per ten seconds for every crawler that honours it (Bing, Yandex; Google ignores it). It also declares news-sitemap.xml, which 404s. NMSA's declared sitemap 404s too, and MSEA declares no sitemap at all.

3. The AI-crawler posture is split down the middle. themortgagepoint.com blocks ten AI crawlers outright — GPTBot, ClaudeBot, anthropic-ai, CCBot, Google-Extended, Applebot-Extended, Amazonbot, meta-externalagent, Bytespider, ImagesiftBot. thefivestar.com blocks none. Blocking is a legitimate publisher choice about uncompensated training; having the two halves of one estate take opposite positions is not a choice, it is drift. Worth deciding deliberately, because it governs whether the industry authority they sell shows up when a buyer asks an AI assistant a mortgage-servicing question.

4. MortgagePoint is, on this evidence, largely invisible to image search. 97% of homepage images have no alt text (37 of 38), 88% on the article, 92% on the podcast hub, 100% on the welcome landing page. That is lost image-search traffic on a media property, and an accessibility exposure on a site that carries advertising.

5. The flagship conference forfeits event rich results. Both Swoogo sites carry no structured data, no canonical, no meta description and no H1. Event schema is the single highest-value markup a conference can have — it is what produces date, venue and ticket rich results in Google. The estate's biggest revenue event has none of it, and neither does the Wild Apricot join page, which is the membership conversion surface.

6. NMSA is marked up as a local business with opening hours. Its JSON-LD emits LocalBusiness + OpeningHoursSpecification for a national trade association — the wrong entity type, telling search engines it is a storefront. AMDC's markup omits an Organization node entirely, so it declares no entity at all. Both are AIOSEO defaults left unconfigured.

7. Thin content and link isolation on exactly the membership properties. The REO Red Book page — a flagship directory product — is 65 words. PPEF's homepage is 98 words, titled "About Us", with four internal links and zero external. AMDC 123, NMSA 186. Against that, FSI pages average ~52 internal links and MortgagePoint's homepage carries 130. The membership sites are near-orphaned islands: too little content to rank, too few links to crawl deeply.

8. Titles waste the most valuable field. MortgagePoint's homepage title is Home - MortgagePoint — 20 characters on a 7,000-follower publication. Its article title runs 99 characters and truncates in results. Seven pages across the estate are titled "Home - X". One page is correctly done: the MortgagePoint article carries BlogPosting + Person author markup, and is the best-optimised page found.

What this does not tell you

"Dark" here means one specific, checkable thing: no HubSpot tracking code was observed in the page source. It does not rule out server-side integrations, scheduled exports, manual uploads, or any other back-channel — none of which is visible from outside.

What tracking absence does establish is narrower and still material: even where records may be synced, behaviour is not. No page view, no session and no source attribution from those surfaces reaches HubSpot. A record that arrives with no acquisition history attached cannot be attributed, whatever else is true.

What the member layer shows

1. No membership group has a people layer at all. LinkedIn showcase pages cannot carry employees — only full company pages have a People tab. So Legal League, FORCE, NMSA, MSEA, PPEF and AMDC have no people associated with them anywhere on LinkedIn. The only way a person connects to a membership group is by typing its name into their own byline, which two people have done for FORCE and nobody has done for the other five.

2. The DS News retirement never propagated to people. Six of the visible MortgagePoint members still name DS News or DSNews.com in their headline — a brand folded into MortgagePoint. The domains were redirected properly; the humans were not. Anyone enriching a contact record from LinkedIn today still gets "DS News" as the employer.

3. The associated-member set is polluted. Four of thirteen visible MortgagePoint "members" are unrelated — an e-commerce founder, a W. L. Gore employee, a university student, a merchant. They are counted because they typed a matching employer string. Apollo.io is already running on the corporate site and MortgagePoint, so contact enrichment is in play — and this is exactly the noise that flows into a CRM through it.

4. The corporate parent is invisible. "Five Star Global" appears only in its own President/CFO's title. It has no LinkedIn page — a company search returns 744 unrelated results. If the legal parent is Five Star Global, then the entity that owns everything is the one entity with no presence.

Why this matters for the HubSpot question

Your model needs holds_role_in to connect a person to a membership group, a community and a media property. The evidence says that edge does not exist in any system observable from outside: not on LinkedIn, where showcase pages cannot hold people; and not in HubSpot, whose tracking is absent from five of six membership sites and from Wild Apricot. Wild Apricot is the only remaining candidate — which makes reading its role and status model the single highest-value next action.

What populating exposed

1. Person was never declared as a class. It appears in two of the ten original edges and in none of the nine classes. The abstract model was internally inconsistent and nobody noticed until there were twenty-three of them to put somewhere. Add it — that makes ten classes.

2. "Status / role" is not a class — it is an edge property. Populating it produced zero nodes and two attributes. "Founding Institutional Partner" is not a thing in the world that exists independently; it is the value on a holds_status_in edge between a person or company and a membership group. Modelling it as a node would create an orphan for every tier. Demote it to an edge attribute.

3. Podcast distribution endpoints are placements, not social channels. Apple, Spotify, Amazon and iHeart are where one media property is exposed — they are not owned presences that publish. Lumping them into Social channel inflated that class and hid the fact that the asset layer's placement concept already covers them exactly. Social channel is 13; the five podcast endpoints belong to the asset model.

4. Three self-referential edges are missing. Organizations have parents (Five Star Global), media properties have successors (DS News → MortgagePoint), and events have sub-events (the Awards Luncheon inside FSC2026). Without them, the retired brands and the corporate parent have nowhere to live — which is exactly why they went missing from the source IA in the first place.
