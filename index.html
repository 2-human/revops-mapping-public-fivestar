The headline. The member-facing directory and join flow run on Wild Apricot, five of six membership-group websites and the flagship conference site carry no HubSpot tracking, and inside the CRM 93.2% of contacts have no digital acquisition source at all. The question "which channel brings us members?" cannot be answered — not because the data is untidy, but because it was never collected.

What the portal changed. August could not tell whether member records nonetheless reached HubSpot by some route invisible from outside. They do — through custom middleware written for this estate, matching on email address. So the correction is narrower and sharper than the original claim: records cross, behaviour does not, and only 2% of contacts carry a membership status at all. Swoogo turned out to be integrated too, writing 3,693 event registrations into the deal object with no pipeline, no stage and no value.

The correspondence map puts every revenue stream, asset and topic against what the CRM holds of it.

The structural observation

MortgagePoint — the advertising-monetized media brand — has 7,000 LinkedIn followers to the institute's 4,000, sits on a full company page rather than a showcase page, and is the best-instrumented property in the estate. The membership groups sit on showcase pages with 119–2,000 followers and mostly dark websites.

On what is observable, the estate is instrumented in inverse proportion to its apparent commercial importance. Media, which monetizes through advertising, is measured well. Membership, which monetizes through dues and events, is barely measured at all. The underlying counts are in Instrumentation.

The portal read supports this from the inside. HubSpot records $3.65M of won deals, and effectively all of it is sponsorship and exhibitor sales. Membership dues reach the CRM as a status string and never as revenue; event registration reaches it as 3,693 deals worth $0.00. The system is configured for the business that sells to organisations, not the one that serves members.

The two boundary questions, answered

1. Wild Apricot does sync — by custom middleware, not a standard integration. Five contact properties carry Wild Apricot data and their own descriptions document the mechanism, naming an edge function that matches records on email address. That is bespoke code running outside both vendors, which is exactly why it is invisible from the outside and why the August review could not find it.

The August conclusion still stands on the part that matters: records cross, behaviour does not. Status, tier, join date and renewal date come across. Not one page view, session or source does.

2. Swoogo also syncs — into the wrong object. It is the portal's largest record producer: 3,693 deals and 2,946 contacts. Every Swoogo deal arrives with no pipeline, no stage, no owner and an amount of $0.00.

This single mechanism explains why 3,613 of 5,110 deals (71%) sit outside every pipeline. Any pipeline metric computed portal-wide is being measured over a population that is 71% non-transactions.

Three findings the outside view could not reach

AM&AA is a second membership business. The member tier list is dominated by AM&AA and CM&AA levels — roughly 3,500 of 4,754 member records, 74% of the member base — and AM&AA owns the second-largest pipeline at $1.29M won. Draft 1 of this review listed seven membership groups; there are eight, and the missing one is larger than the other seven combined. It also resolves what Five Star Global is: the parent holding both brands. That was previously evidenced only by one executive's job title.

Membership status is recorded three times and the three disagree. Nine per-group status properties, a Wild Apricot status that declares itself the source of truth, and a legacy property described in its own configuration as "To be archived Q2 2026" — still live and still populated in September. No precedence is declared anywhere. Asked how many active members there are, the portal returns 1,763 or 2,293 depending which model you read. The per-group picture is starker still: FORCE reads 2,003 lapsed against 227 active.

The deal object is carrying event attendance. Alongside the 3,693 Swoogo deals, roughly 2.38M notes record the same fact in prose. 99% of those notes date from a 2020 migration, but the practice continues — a note created during this audit reads "Registered for Under the Microscope: How PE is Buying and Building in Healthcare - Austin Chapter Event". Event attendance is therefore recorded twice, and neither form can be reported on.

What could not be read

Four things are closed to this audit, and they bound every conclusion above:

  • Workflows. Not exposed by the connector at all. Every statement here about automation is inferred from its output, never read directly. This is the largest single gap.
  • p8733520_enrollments — the portal's only custom object, and on its name the likely home of membership or course records. Needs reauthorisation.
  • Forms and marketing emails. 16,021 contacts originate from forms whose definitions cannot be read; the email channel cannot be assessed.
  • Saved reports and dashboards. Disabled for this portal, so what the organisation currently reports on — and the gap between that and the figures above — is unknown.

Three of the four close with one reauthorisation and one permission change. Workflows and saved reports need a screen share.

What it turned up

1. FORCE's entire member-news content type silently redirects to the homepage. fivestarforce.com/member-news/update-your-reo-red-book-profile/ — a URL Google still indexes and links to — returns 200 OK on the homepage, with the canonical pointing at fivestarforce.com/. Not a 404, not a 301 to a replacement: a soft-404 catch-all. The observable consequence is that a visitor arriving from search lands somewhere unrelated to what they clicked; the likely SEO consequence is that ranking equity for those URLs consolidates into the homepage rather than the intended pages. One URL was tested, so whether the whole content type behaves this way is inferred from the catch-all pattern, not proven across it.

2. The corporate site throttles its own crawling. thefivestar.com/robots.txt sets Crawl-delay: 10 — one page per ten seconds for every crawler that honours it (Bing, Yandex; Google ignores it). It also declares news-sitemap.xml, which 404s. NMSA's declared sitemap 404s too, and MSEA declares no sitemap at all.

3. The AI-crawler posture is split down the middle. themortgagepoint.com blocks ten AI crawlers outright — GPTBot, ClaudeBot, anthropic-ai, CCBot, Google-Extended, Applebot-Extended, Amazonbot, meta-externalagent, Bytespider, ImagesiftBot. thefivestar.com blocks none. Blocking is a legitimate publisher choice about uncompensated training; having the two halves of one estate take opposite positions is not a choice, it is drift. Worth deciding deliberately, because it governs whether the industry authority they sell shows up when a buyer asks an AI assistant a mortgage-servicing question.

4. MortgagePoint is, on this evidence, largely invisible to image search. 97% of homepage images have no alt text (37 of 38), 88% on the article, 92% on the podcast hub, 100% on the welcome landing page. That is lost image-search traffic on a media property, and an accessibility exposure on a site that carries advertising.

5. The flagship conference forfeits event rich results. Both Swoogo sites carry no structured data, no canonical, no meta description and no H1. Event schema is the single highest-value markup a conference can have — it is what produces date, venue and ticket rich results in Google. The estate's biggest revenue event has none of it, and neither does the Wild Apricot join page, which is the membership conversion surface.

6. NMSA is marked up as a local business with opening hours. Its JSON-LD emits LocalBusiness + OpeningHoursSpecification for a national trade association — the wrong entity type, telling search engines it is a storefront. AMDC's markup omits an Organization node entirely, so it declares no entity at all. Both are AIOSEO defaults left unconfigured.

7. Thin content and link isolation on exactly the membership properties. The REO Red Book page — a flagship directory product — is 65 words. PPEF's homepage is 98 words, titled "About Us", with four internal links and zero external. AMDC 123, NMSA 186. Against that, FSI pages average ~52 internal links and MortgagePoint's homepage carries 130. The membership sites are near-orphaned islands: too little content to rank, too few links to crawl deeply.

8. Titles waste the most valuable field. MortgagePoint's homepage title is Home - MortgagePoint — 20 characters on a 7,000-follower publication. Its article title runs 99 characters and truncates in results. Seven pages across the estate are titled "Home - X". One page is correctly done: the MortgagePoint article carries BlogPosting + Person author markup, and is the best-optimised page found.

What populating exposed, and what changed

All five are applied. The class list above is the revised model.

1. Person was never declared as a class. It appears in two of the ten original edges and in none of the nine classes. The abstract model was internally inconsistent and nobody noticed until there were twenty-three of them to put somewhere. Added.

2. "Status / role" is not a class — it is an edge property. Populating it produced zero nodes and two attributes. "Founding Institutional Partner" is not a thing in the world that exists independently; it is the value on a holds_status_in edge between a person or company and a membership group. Modelling it as a node would create an orphan for every tier. Removed as a class; it is now the status attribute on that edge.

3. Podcast distribution endpoints are placements, not social channels. Apple, Spotify, Amazon and iHeart are where one media property is exposed — they are not owned presences that publish. Lumping them into Social channel inflated that class and hid the fact that the asset layer's placement concept already covers them exactly. Social channel is 13; the five podcast endpoints belong to the asset model. Moved to Placement.

4. Three self-referential edges were missing. Organizations have parents (Five Star Global), media properties have successors (DS News → MortgagePoint), and events have sub-events (the Awards Luncheon inside FSC2026). Without them, the retired brands and the corporate parent have nowhere to live — which is exactly why they went missing from the source IA in the first place. Present in the edge list above.

5. Placement had the same defect as Person, and applying refinement 3 exposed it. Moving the podcast endpoints out of Social channel meant naming where they went — and Placement turned out to be referenced by the distributed_to edge while appearing in no class list. Two of the ten classes were undeclared, and both were discoverable only by trying to put something in them. Declared, with 5 instances.

The pattern worth carrying forward. Both undeclared classes sat in the edge list and not the class list. An IA drafted class-first will keep doing this, because edges get written to describe a relationship without checking that both ends exist. A validation rule that reads every edge and asserts both endpoints are declared classes would have caught Person and Placement before either was populated.

What this draft does not cover

Stated plainly rather than left for a reader to discover. Everything below is a known gap, not an omission.

Closed by the portal read

  • Membership tiers — now known. 33 tiers are enumerated in the CRM, synced from Wild Apricot.
  • Does Wild Apricot reach HubSpot? — answered. Records do, by custom middleware matched on email; behaviour does not.
  • Five Star Alliance — it is a group, not a tier. It carries a per-group status property of the same shape as the confirmed groups, under the internal name "FS Access".
  • Five Star Global — the parent entity, holding Five Star Institute and AM&AA. Previously evidenced only by one executive's job title.

Still open

  • Communities — the four are listed on the site, but no community property exists in HubSpot at all, so member-to-community assignment is not recorded anywhere the audit could reach.
  • News & Resources — not crawled.
  • Webinars — referenced as a media type; no platform identified. A Zoom integration writes to contacts, which is a lead but not a confirmation.
  • Legal League Servicer Summit — unconfirmed as a current event.
  • jobs.thefivestar.com and fsireferrals.com — need browser verification; both refused non-browser clients.
  • Follower counts for X, Facebook and YouTube were not captured.
  • Member coverage is capped. LinkedIn gates employee visibility behind Sales Navigator — 10 of 40 and 13 of 50 were visible. The people layer is a sample, not a roster.
  • On-page SEO characterises templates, not pages. One page per page type was checked, so site-wide issues generalise and per-article issues do not.
  • Duplicate records were not measured. The query surface available to this audit has no text functions, so email-level duplication could not be counted. Two byte-identical event deals were found by inspection, which suggests it is worth measuring properly.
  • Wild Apricot's own side was not read. HubSpot holds 4,754 member records. Whether that is the whole membership or a partial sync cannot be settled from HubSpot alone.

Closed to the audit inside the portal

  • Workflows — not exposed by the connector at all. Every statement about automation on this page is inferred from its output. The largest single gap.
  • p8733520_enrollments — the portal's only custom object, and on its name the likely home of membership or course records. Needs reauthorisation.
  • Forms and marketing emails — 16,021 contacts originate from forms whose definitions cannot be read.
  • Saved reports and dashboards — disabled for this portal, so what the organisation currently reports on is unknown. The gap between that and section 3 is likely the most useful unknown remaining.

The one change that would open the most

Three of the four portal gaps close with one reauthorisation and one permission change on the connected HubSpot user — the enrollments object, forms, and marketing emails. Workflows and saved reports need a screen share instead. Nothing else on this list is blocked by anything as small.
